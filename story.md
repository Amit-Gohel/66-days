# Start Here — The Story of This Diary

*You've just opened a field-diary. The cover is worn like it belongs to someone who has kept it for years. But every page is blank, and the name on the first one is yours.*

*This document is the only part written for you to read once. Everything after it, you write yourself. Take ten minutes with this, and you'll know exactly what you're holding and exactly what to do with it.*

---

## The diary you just picked up

Plainly, before any storytelling: this is a **66-day training program for your own attention and judgment**, run as a web app that looks and feels like a private paper journal. You log in, jot a few small notes during the day, sit down once at night to think them through, and review the week on Sundays. Do that, and over roughly two months the app quietly turns your scattered observations into a measurable record of how sharp your perception and forecasting actually are.

It trains four things, and only these four:

- **Perception** — noticing what's normal somewhere, so what's *abnormal* stands out.
- **Judgment** — reasoning that you can check against reality instead of just trusting.
- **People-reading** — reading comfort and discomfort, asking better questions — *never* "detecting lies."
- **Calibrated forecasting** — putting numbers on your beliefs and scoring them, so you learn whether your gut is any good.

That's the whole promise. Notice what it does *not* say: it does not promise to make you smarter in general, faster at everything, or a human lie-detector. The diary is unusually honest about this, and that honesty is the point — we'll come back to it.

If you remember one sentence: **you capture raw material by day, you make sense of it at night, and the system keeps the score so you can see yourself improving.**

## Who the hero is (it's you)

Here is the quiet problem this was built for.

Most of us move through our days on autopilot. We see a room and remember almost none of it. We form strong opinions and never find out if they were right. We "have a good feeling" about a person, a deal, a deadline — and we never check the feeling against what happened. Our judgment might be excellent or it might be badly miscalibrated, and the honest truth is **we have no idea, because we never keep score.**

You are the one this diary is for. Not because you're broken — because you're operating without instruments. A pilot doesn't trust the feeling of level flight; they trust the artificial horizon. This is an artificial horizon for your perception and judgment. You are the pilot. The diary is only the instrument panel.

So as you read on, don't look for a guru telling you what to think. Look for a guide handing you a plan and a set of gauges. The hero of these 66 days is you. The app just refuses to let you fly blind.

## Why you can trust the guide

Most self-improvement apps ask for your faith. This one shows its work.

Every technique inside it carries a small **evidence chip** — a little label that tells you, honestly, *how well-proven the thing you're about to do actually is*:

- **`[Confirmed]`** — backed by solid peer-reviewed research. (Retrieval practice. Calibrated forecasting. Implementation intentions.)
- **`[Practitioner]`** — used in real professional doctrine, but not cleanly proven in controlled studies. (The After-Action Review. Baseline-and-anomaly observation.)
- **`[Inference]`** — a reasonable extension of the evidence.
- **`[Speculative]`** — plausible, but unverified.
- **`[CONTESTED]`** — popular but empirically weak, and handled with deliberate care.

Tap any chip and it shows you the actual source. The People Note, for instance, is labeled `[CONTESTED]` on purpose — right next to it the app reminds you that humans detect lies at about 54%, barely above a coin flip, so you are told to record *observations, not verdicts*. A system that argues against its own most seductive feature is a system you can trust with the rest.

This whole program was built by auditing three independent research drafts against their primary sources, claim by claim, and keeping only what survived. It is described, in its own words, as *"a well-reasoned bet"* — not a miracle. Your guide is the kind that tells you the odds.

## The plan is three moves

For all its depth, the daily rhythm is just three moves. Hold these and the rest is detail.

**1 — Capture, by day (about 5 minutes, scattered).**
As life happens, you jot raw material into the diary. You do *not* analyze it yet. Analysis in the moment ruins observation; you just collect.

**2 — Synthesize, at night (one quiet block).**
At a fixed cue — *"after I brush my teeth"* — you open the **night session**: a calm, full-screen, lamplit ritual that walks you, one step at a time, through making sense of the day. This is where the actual thinking happens. In your first two weeks it takes about five minutes. Later it settles around half an hour.

**3 — Review, on Sundays (about half an hour).**
Once a week you re-read, tally your predictions, cull your best idea, and look for patterns that showed up three or more times. This is where memory and calibration compound.

That's the engine. Capture → synthesize → review. Everything else in the app exists to serve those three moves or to show you the results of them.

## Day 1, minute by minute — exactly what to do

This is the part for the moment you're staring at the screen not knowing where to click. Follow it literally.

### Sign in, and set your ritual

After you create an account, the app asks you three short questions. This is the **onboarding**, and it matters more than it looks.

1. **It states the goal** — sharper perception, calibrated forecasting, observation over conclusion. It also says, in plain type, *"Domain-specific gains, not an IQ boost."* Read that line and believe it.
2. **It asks you to set your nightly ritual.** You'll complete the sentence: *"After I ___, I will open the journal."* Anchor it to something you already do every single day without fail — brushing your teeth, making coffee, locking the front door. This one choice is the **single biggest lever** for whether you'll still be here on Day 30. Choose a cue that genuinely never gets skipped.
3. **It asks for a start date** — today, tomorrow, or a date you pick. Then it tells you the rule of Phase 1: *miss one day and nothing resets; miss two in a row and the streak resets.*

Press the button that says **"Day 1 awaits."** You're in.

### The diary hub (Home)

You land on **Home** — your diary's front page. Don't be daunted; on Day 1 most of it is intentionally quiet. You'll see:

- **The day and phase** at the top (*Day 1 · Phase 1*), and a line reminding you of your cue: *"your cue → tonight's session."*
- **Today's captures** — a small set of cards. In Phase 1, only two are open: **D1 Baseline + Anomaly** and **D2 Three Noticings**. The others are dimmed and locked. This is on purpose — the program refuses to overwhelm you early.
- **A "Begin night session" button** — your evening destination.
- On the right: a **stats card** (your Craft Points, streak, freezes), a **word count** of everything you've written, and **the 66-day diary** — a heatmap of 66 cells, one per day, that fills in as you go. Right now it's almost entirely empty. That empty grid is the story you're about to write.

**Your two daytime captures, in plain terms:**

- **D1 — Baseline + Anomaly.** Pick one place you were today. Write what's *normal* there (the baseline), then what *broke* the pattern (the anomaly), then a one-line "so what." *Example: "Coffee shop, 8am. Baseline: regulars, laptops, quiet. Anomaly: a man in a winter coat though it's warm, watching the door, didn't order. So what: probably waiting — low threat, but I noted the exit."* You can't notice what's off until you've named what's normal. That's the entire skill.
- **D2 — Three Noticings.** Three concrete, specific things you observed. Detail, not interpretation. *"The barista re-stacks cups left-handed. Three people wore the same trainers. Street noise dropped sharply at 9:10."*

Tap a card, write a sentence or two, and it autosaves. That's a capture. It takes a minute. Do it whenever something catches your eye — you don't sit down for it.

### The night session — the heart of it

This is the move that makes the whole thing work, so don't skip it. When your cue fires (you brushed your teeth), open **Begin night session**.

The screen goes dark and full-screen — a lamplit room, one step at a time, a small "~minutes left" in the corner. In **Phase 1 it is deliberately tiny**: a single two-line review. It asks *"What happened?"* and then *"Improve — as an if-then."* You write something like: *"Rushed the school run, forgot the permission slip. If it's a school morning, then I lay the bag by the door the night before."* That's it. Two lines.

When you finish, the diary says: **"Session logged. Tonight's synthesis is saved. The streak holds."** Then: **"Return to the diary."**

That sentence — *the streak holds* — is your Day 1 win. You showed up. The cue worked. You can stop there and the day is complete.

As the weeks pass, that same night session grows new steps — recalling the day from memory before you peek, logging a prediction, arguing against your own belief, developing an idea — but each one arrives only when you're ready for it. Tonight, two lines is the whole job.

## How the diary grows with you

The program is built in **five phases** across 66 days. It starts almost insultingly easy on purpose — habit science says lock in the *cue* first and add difficulty only once the behavior is sticky. New tools *unlock* as you earn the right to them, the way a roadmap reveals the next stretch only as you reach it.

- **Phase 1 · Days 1–14 — Install the anchor.** Only D1, D2, and the two-line night review. The single goal is *never miss*. Nothing else matters yet.
- **Phase 2 · Days 15–28 — Add precision.** The night session grows: recall-from-memory, the **calibrated prediction log**, the People Note, the full four-question After-Action Review. Your first **weekly review** appears around Day 21. Predictions start now so there's a track record to read by Day 66.
- **Phase 3 · Days 29–44 — Add rigor.** Idea seeds, "consider-the-opposite" to fight your own confirmation bias, SCAMPER for developing ideas, Brier scoring of your forecasts, and a small "drill of the day." Around Day 35 you cut anything you keep skipping.
- **Phase 4 · Days 45–60 — Transfer & calibration.** You push the skills into real conversations, add spaced "what happened 7 and 30 days ago" recall sweeps, and your **calibration analytics** come fully alive.
- **Phase 5 · Days 61–66 — Automaticity check.** The load *tapers*. You chart your calibration curve and your anomaly miss-rate, pick your top three ideas of the whole 66 days, run a final review on the system itself, and design the lean version you'll keep afterward.

Two rules carry you the whole way:

- **Never miss twice.** Missing a single day does not reset you — that's real habit science, not leniency. Only chronic, back-to-back skipping derails a habit. So if you miss, the only job is: don't miss tomorrow.
- **There is always a non-negotiable core.** On your busiest, worst day, the minimum is one baseline-and-anomaly note, one idea, and the two-line review. Everything else is optional load. The program is designed to survive your real life.

## How you'll know it's actually working

This is the question most self-help can't answer, and it's the one the diary takes most seriously. You will get **two honest gauges**, not vibes.

**Your calibration curve.** Every prediction you log gets a probability — *"client signs by Friday, 65%"* — and a resolution date. When the date comes, you mark it hit or miss inside the night session. Over weeks, the **Calibration** screen draws the curve: *when you say 70%, do those things actually happen about 70% of the time?* A perfectly calibrated forecaster sits on the diagonal. You'll see, in a chart you can't argue with, whether you're overconfident, underconfident, or sharp. Alongside it: your **Brier score** (lower is better — superforecasters average around 0.166), its trend over time, and your hit rate.

**Your anomaly miss-rate.** Each night, before you look at your captures, you recall the day from memory. Then you reveal what you wrote and mark what you *forgot*. The things you keep forgetting are your perception blind spots, named and tracked. Across 66 days that miss-rate should fall.

This is the real reason the program is 66 days and not 50: calibration needs weeks of resolved forecasts before the numbers mean anything. The extra time isn't padding — it's how the gauges earn their accuracy.

## The quiet companion (points, streaks, and badges — done right)

The app has a game layer, but it was designed *against* the manipulative kind, deliberately. Its whole philosophy is one rule: **mechanics that affirm your competence and celebrate real progress help; mechanics that create pressure, deadlines, or loss-threats undermine the very motivation you're here to build.** So here's what you'll see, and what it will never do:

- **Craft Points** reflect the work you've actually done — a small amount for each capture, more for a completed night session. They are a *mirror of effort*, never a currency. There is no store. You can't spend them, and nothing is ever locked behind them.
- **The forgiving streak.** "Never miss twice," exactly as on the tin. And completing a weekly review **earns you a streak freeze** (up to two banked) that quietly absorbs a missed day. Freezes are *earned, never purchasable* — there is no "pay to restore," no countdown timer, no "you'll lose it tonight" guilt. By design, it is structurally impossible to ever sell you back your own progress.
- **Badges** mark genuine milestones tied to the method — your first completed night session, a seven-day streak, ten predictions resolved, reaching each phase, your first developed idea, Day 66. Once earned they are never taken away. A brief, calm celebration appears (and respects reduced-motion settings — no flashing).
- **The Cohort** (an optional leaderboard and a cooperative "buddy streak") is **off by default**. If you ever turn it on, it shares only your display name and points — *never a word of what you wrote* — and the buddy streak is purely cooperative: one person's gap never resets the other's. No leagues, no demotion drama, no notifications nagging you about your rank.

If a feature ever feels like it's pressuring you, that's a bug against this design, not the intent. The companion is meant to be *quiet*.

## The honest contract — what to expect, and what not to

The diary makes you a few promises and refuses to make several others. Hold it to all of them.

**What it will give you,** if you show up: sharper observation, more structured reasoning, measurably better calibration, and more attentive, more honest people-reading. These are *domain-specific* gains in the things you actually practice.

**What it will not give you:** a general intelligence boost, "rewired" cognition, or any ability to detect lies from body language. The research is decisive that broad far-transfer doesn't happen and that behavioral lie-detection doesn't work, and the app would rather tell you that than sell you a fantasy.

**About "66 days":** it's a real number from real research — but it's an *average*, drawn from a range that ran anywhere from 18 to 254 days. Day 66 is not a magic finish line where a habit unlocks. It's the point where you'll have enough data to *measure* yourself and design what you keep. The cue should feel automatic well before then; the full routine may still take effort on Day 66. That's normal.

**The bottom line, in the system's own honest words:** every individual component is evidence-backed or doctrine-grade and labeled as such — but the bundled protocol has never been tested as a single unit. So treat it as *a well-reasoned bet*. Your calibration curve and your miss-rate are how you'll know whether the bet is paying off for *you*.

## Your first five minutes, right now

If you read nothing else, do exactly this:

1. **Create your account and finish onboarding.** Spend real thought on the cue — *"after I ___, I will open the journal."* Pick a daily habit you never skip.
2. **Right now, log one D1.** Think of one place you were today. Write its baseline, the one thing that broke it, and a one-line "so what." Sixty seconds.
3. **Tonight, when your cue fires, open the night session.** In Phase 1 it's two lines: what happened, and one if-then improvement. Finish it and read the words *"The streak holds."*
4. **Tomorrow, do it again.** That's the whole secret. The depth, the predictions, the charts, the badges — they all arrive on their own. Your only job for the next two weeks is to keep the cue.

The one way to fail is to treat this as a streak to protect rather than a practice to do — to chase the number instead of doing the work, or to skip the night session because the day's captures felt like "enough." They aren't the work; the night is. Show up for the night, even for two lines, and the system does the rest.
