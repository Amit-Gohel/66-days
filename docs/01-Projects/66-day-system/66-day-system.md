# The 66-Day Perception & Judgment System
### One verified system, merged from three audited drafts and re-checked against primary sources

*An audit-and-merge of three independent 50-day plans, re-verified claim-by-claim against the original research and doctrine, then redesigned around the ~66-day habit-formation horizon.*

**Evidence labels used throughout:**
**[Confirmed]** = backed by peer-reviewed research · **[Practitioner]** = used in professional doctrine but not cleanly proven in controlled studies · **[Inference]** = reasonable extrapolation from the evidence · **[Speculative]** = plausible but unverified · **[CONTESTED]** = popular but empirically weak — handled with care.

Every empirical figure below was independently re-checked against a primary or authoritative source during this audit (June 2026). The full claim-by-claim ledger is in **Appendix A**.

---

## PART 1 — VALIDATION SUMMARY

### What all three drafts agreed on, and got right
The three drafts independently converged on the same skeleton, and the evidence supports it:

- **Two-zone architecture** — light *capture* during the day, structured *synthesis* at night. All three landed here; it is the correct call (see Part 2's structure hypotheses).
- **Observation = Baseline + Anomaly**, from USMC Combat Hunter doctrine via *Left of Bang* (Van Horne & Riley, 2014), mapped onto Endsley's three-level situation-awareness model (1995). **[Confirmed** model **/ Practitioner** drill**]**
- **Reflection = the After-Action Review** (US Army *TC 25-20*, 1993). **[Practitioner]**
- **Reasoning = calibrated forecasting with Brier scoring** (Tetlock's Good Judgment Project). This is the single best-evidenced reasoning drill in the system. **[Confirmed]**
- **Micro-expression lie-detection is unreliable** and must be excluded. All three flagged it; the evidence is decisive (see below). **[CONTESTED/debunked]**

### The key conflicts, and how I resolved each
1. **Is "66 days" a mean or a median?** It is a **mean** — Draft 3 was right, Draft 1's "median" is a common misreport. Lally herself: "the average time (among those for whom our model was a good fit) was 66 days." Resolution below in *Is 66 days justified?*
2. **The sixth Combat Hunter domain.** Draft 2 listed "**Heuristics**" — this is **wrong**. The six domains in *Left of Bang* are Kinesics, Biometrics, Proxemics, Geographics, **Iconography**, and Atmospherics (Draft 3 correct). Fixed throughout.
3. **Creativity — keep or cut?** Draft 2 deleted idea-generation and replaced it with hypothesis analysis. But creativity is an explicit target skill, and solo idea generation is *evidence-backed* (Diehl & Stroebe, 1987 — individuals out-produce brainstorming groups). **Kept.** Cutting it was an error.
4. **How central should ACH (Analysis of Competing Hypotheses) be?** Draft 2 made a daily "Micro-ACH" the engine and claimed it "dismantles bias." The controlled evidence does **not** support that: experimental tests find ACH has "little to no overall benefit on judgment quality, and may even harm it" (Dhami & Mandel, 2019; Mandel et al. critical review, 2024). **Resolution:** demote ACH to an occasional, lightweight two-hypothesis check and lead de-biasing with the better-supported **"consider-the-opposite"** strategy (Lord, Lepper & Preston, 1984). Draft 3 was right to flag this.
5. **Elicitation ethics.** Draft 2 prescribed deploying **deliberate false statements** and "ego-up / ego-down" manipulation on colleagues *daily*. The Scharff technique is real and validated *for intelligence elicitation* — but daily manipulation of people you actually know is ethically corrosive and unsustainable, and it isn't necessary to train the underlying skill. **Resolution:** adopt Draft 3's framing — mostly *notice* elicitation in the wild and use benign, honest conversational moves (labels, calibrated questions) sparingly.
6. **Daily networking/outreach.** A LinkedIn-style outreach is not a perception skill, and forcing it daily threatens the core habit. **Resolution:** make it conditional, value-first, with a weekly (not daily) target.
7. **How much will this transfer?** Draft 2 implied broad cognitive "rewiring." The brain-training literature is decisively negative on far transfer (Simons et al., 2016; Owen et al., 2010). **Resolution:** promise **domain-specific** gains — sharper observation, more structured reasoning, better calibration, more attentive people-reading — not a general IQ boost. Draft 3's honesty here was the correct posture.

### Claims the drafts got wrong, overstated, or couldn't support
- **"Heuristics" as a Combat Hunter domain** (Draft 2) — wrong; it's Iconography.
- **ACH "dismantles bias"** (Draft 2) — overstated; controlled evidence is weak-to-negative.
- **Broad neuroplastic "rewiring" / general transfer** (Draft 2's framing) — unsupported; gains are domain-specific.
- **"66 days" as a median or a fixed threshold** (Draft 1 wording; widespread) — it's a soft *mean* with an enormous 18–254-day range.
- **"Six principles" of Cialdini** (all drafts) — there are now **seven**; "Unity" was added in the 2021 *New and Expanded* edition.
- **Heavy reliance on Wikipedia / Scribd / Goodreads** as if primary (Draft 2's citations) — the underlying doctrine is sound, but those are not primary sources; the brief asked for primary works.
- **The "4-question AAR"** (all drafts) is a popular simplification of fuller *TC 25-20* doctrine — fine to use, but it's a compression, not the verbatim manual.
- Minor: Draft 3's exact Roediger & Karpicke percentages and its attribution of the "10–20% spacing" rule to Cepeda *2006* (that specific ratio is from Cepeda et al. *2008*) — directionally correct, slightly imprecise.

Notably, **Draft 2's one citation that proved *more* accurate than the others** was its doctrinal sourcing: MCIP 3-02.1i *Combat Hunter* (2011) was indeed cancelled and folded into **MCTP 3-01A *Scouting and Patrolling* (July 2020)** — so that is the current military primary source, with *Left of Bang* as the civilian-accessible version.

### Is the 66-day extension justified? — Yes, with an honest asterisk
The figure is real and correctly sourced to **Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle (2010)**, *European Journal of Social Psychology*. But the audit sharpens three things the drafts blurred:

- It is a **mean of ~66 days to reach the automaticity plateau**, calculated **only across the subset of participants whose behavior fit the model's curve** — not a universal constant.
- The range was **18 to 254 days**. Sixty-six is the middle of a very wide distribution, *not* a finish line.
- The behaviors studied were **simple, once-daily health actions** (e.g., drinking water, eating fruit). A multi-component cognitive journal is more demanding, so honest expectation-setting says: the *core cue* should feel automatic well within 66 days, while the *fuller routine* may still take effort on Day 66. **[Confirmed** figure **/ Inference** to apply it here**]**

**So the redesign does not treat Day 66 as a magic threshold.** It uses the 66-day horizon to do what 50 days cannot: (a) let a **prediction track record accumulate** long enough that your calibration is actually measurable; (b) open up **longer spacing intervals** for re-testing earlier observations (the spacing effect rewards longer gaps for longer retention — Cepeda et al., 2006/2008); (c) add **transfer / in-vivo reps**; and (d) run a real **meta-review**. The extra 16 days *deepen and space* the work; they are not filler. Two supporting habit findings make 66 days workable: implementation intentions (a fixed if-then cue) raise follow-through with a medium-to-large effect (d ≈ 0.65; Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006), and **missing one day does not derail the process** — only chronic inconsistency does (Lally et al., 2010). **[Confirmed]**

## PART 2 — THE FINAL 66-DAY SYSTEM (the daily sheet)

### The structure decision (journal-architecture hypotheses)
I weighed three structures against the time budget:

- **Hypothesis A — Capture-only minimalist** (jot things freeform). *Confidence it's optimal: LOW.* Lowest friction, but no retrieval, no feedback, no compounding — you accumulate notes you never reuse.
- **Hypothesis B — Fixed structured night sheet only.** *Confidence: MEDIUM.* Matches AAR doctrine and forces the skills, but it's heavy, has no spacing tier, and risks abandonment.
- **Hypothesis C — Three tiers: light daytime capture + structured night session + weekly spaced review.** *Confidence: HIGH.* **This is the recommendation.** It fits "scattered 5-min slots + one 30–45 min session," and the weekly tier is where the actual memory mechanisms — spacing (Cepeda) and retrieval (Roediger & Karpicke) — live. **[Inference** from combining AAR doctrine with the spacing/testing literature**]**

Times below are targets; the night session is engineered to land at **~32–38 minutes** on a full day and under 45 on its heaviest.

---

### ZONE 1 — DAYTIME MICRO-CAPTURES  (3–4 scattered slots, ~5 min total)
*You are only capturing raw material during the day — not analyzing. Analysis happens at night. This protects the time budget and exploits the fact that observation degrades when you try to interpret in real time.*

#### D1 — Baseline + Anomaly  (observation / situational awareness)
- **Technique & source:** Baseline + Anomaly observation from USMC Combat Hunter doctrine — **MCTP 3-01A *Scouting and Patrolling* (USMC, 2020)**, popularized in **Van Horne & Riley, *Left of Bang* (2014)**; framed by **Endsley, "Toward a Theory of Situation Awareness in Dynamic Systems," *Human Factors* (1995)**. **[Practitioner** drill **/ Confirmed** SA model**]**
- **Mechanism:** Experts don't see *more*; they hold a sharper **baseline** of "normal," so deviations pop out. *Left of Bang*'s equation is **Baseline + Anomaly → Decision**, across six domains (kinesics, biometrics, proxemics, geographics, iconography, atmospherics). Logging anomalies forces you to first encode the baseline — Endsley's Level 1 (perception); you can't comprehend (L2) or project (L3) what you never perceived.
- **Time:** ~2 min, once or twice.
- **What to write:** One place/situation → (1) Baseline: what's normal here? (2) Anomaly: what broke it? (3) So what? — a one-line projection.
- **Filled example:** *"Coffee shop, 8am. Baseline: regulars, laptops, quiet. Anomaly: man in a winter coat (it's warm), watching the door, didn't order. So what: probably waiting/anxious — low threat, but I noted the exit."*
- **Compounds over 66 days:** You bank dozens of baselines for recurring environments, so anomaly detection becomes faster and increasingly pre-attentive — the doctrine's explicit endpoint. The weekly review applies the **"Rule of Three"** (three anomalies warrant a decision) to spot real patterns vs. noise.

#### D2 — Three Noticings  (observation / concrete detail)
- **Technique & source:** Attention-priming via a pre-committed if-then intention (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006); the observation-training tradition of Amy Herman's *Visual Intelligence* (2016). **[Confirmed** mechanism **/ Inference** as a drill**]**
- **Mechanism:** Committing *in advance* to record three specific things is an implementation intention; it raises the mental accessibility of cues so you see more. Demanding **concrete detail, not judgment**, trains perception rather than storytelling.
- **Time:** ~1–2 min.
- **What to write:** Three unusual/interesting things — sensory, behavioral, environmental. Detail over interpretation.
- **Filled example:** *"1. Barista re-stacks cups left-handed. 2. Three people wore the same brand of trainers. 3. Street noise drops sharply at 9:10."*
- **Compounds:** Trains noticing concrete particulars rather than gist — the raw material pattern recognition feeds on. Distinct from the "Three Good Things" gratitude exercise (which targets mood, not perception).

#### D3 — One Problem → Idea Seed  (creativity)
- **Technique & source:** Solo divergent production — **Osborn, *Applied Imagination* (1953)** ("quantity breeds quality"); **Diehl & Stroebe (1987)** showed individuals generating alone out-produce interactive brainstorming groups (production blocking). Problem-first framing follows Getzels & Csikszentmihalyi's problem-finding work. **[Confirmed]**
- **Mechanism:** Quantity is a legitimate route to quality at the *corpus* level (you cull later); a daily quota lowers self-censorship and builds fluency. Honest caveat: at the *single-idea* level, quantity→quality is moderated by Openness and is mixed (Forthmann et al., 2019) — so optimize for **volume + weekly selection**, not daily brilliance.
- **Time:** ~2 min.
- **What to write:** State a **problem first** ("X is annoying because…"), then one candidate solution; anchor it to something you actually observed that day.
- **Filled example:** *"Problem: gym lockers jam at 6pm. Idea: cheap door-sensors + an app showing live locker availability."*
- **Compounds:** ~66 seeds → weekly cull to the best one → ~9 developed ideas by Day 66, plus a trained reflex for spotting friction. Most seeds will be weak; that is expected and correct.

#### D4 — One People Note: Baseline → Shift → Question  (social / emotional awareness)
- **Technique & source:** Nonverbal **baselining** from **Navarro, *What Every Body Is Saying* (2008)** — read **comfort/discomfort** and "pacifying behaviors," *never* lie detection — bounded by the deception evidence below. **[Practitioner**, with **[CONTESTED]** boundaries explicit**]**
- **Mechanism & its hard limit:** Reading deviation from a *person's own* baseline tells you *where to ask*, not what's true. Lie-detection from behavior is not reliable: Bond & DePaulo (2006) — 206 studies, 24,483 judges — found **54% accuracy**, and DePaulo et al. (2003) found behavioral cues to deception are "faint and unreliable." Record **observations, not verdicts.**
- **Time:** ~1–2 min.
- **What to write:** Person → their baseline → the shift (comfort↔discomfort) and around *what topic* → the open question it makes you want to ask.
- **Filled example:** *"Colleague (baseline: leans in, fast talker) went quiet and crossed arms when budget came up. Discomfort around that topic. Don't conclude 'hiding something' — next time ask: 'What's your read on the budget?'"*
- **Compounds:** Builds a calibrated, baseline-aware library of the *specific* people you deal with — the only valid form of "reading people."

> *Conditional, not daily — Outreach (social capital):* When a **genuine, value-first** reason exists, send one give-first message (Cialdini's reciprocity/liking; *Influence*, 1984/2021). Target **2–3 per week, tracked separately** — never a daily streak item. Each one is a live rep for a Voss label or calibrated question. **[Confirmed** principles **/ Inference** as a drill**]**

### ZONE 2 — NIGHT SESSION  (one block, ~32–38 min, at a fixed cue)
*Do it at a fixed cue every night (e.g., "after I brush my teeth") — the implementation-intention trigger that makes it stick (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006, d ≈ 0.65). [Confirmed]*

#### N1 — Retrieval-First Recall  (memory) — ~3–5 min
- **Technique & source:** The **testing effect / retrieval practice** — **Roediger & Karpicke, "Test-Enhanced Learning," *Psychological Science* (2006)**. **[Confirmed]**
- **Mechanism:** Pulling memory from a *blank page* strengthens it far more than re-reading — especially at a delay of days (re-reading wins only at ~5 minutes, and it inflates confidence without improving later recall). So **before opening your captures, recall the day from memory**, then reconcile.
- **What to write:** Freehand everything you remember (anomalies, idea, interaction); then open captures and mark what you **missed** — missed items are your perception blind spots.
- **Filled example:** *"Recalled 2 of 3 anomalies + the locker idea. Forgot the colleague's budget reaction entirely — I under-encode work-social cues. Flag."*
- **Compounds:** A visible record of *what you keep forgetting* directs attention; your miss-rate should fall across 66 days — track it.

#### N2 — The After-Action Review  (reflection / reasoning) — ~8–10 min
- **Technique & source:** **US Army *TC 25-20, A Leader's Guide to After-Action Reviews* (1993)** (compressed four-question form). **[Practitioner;** reflection-improves-performance mechanism **Confirmed]**
- **Mechanism:** A disciplined, **blameless** comparison of intended vs. actual that ends in concrete sustain/improve lists — it converts raw experience into a retrievable lesson (mere experience without feedback does not; Ericsson et al., 1993). Keep it analytic, not self-flagellating (rumination that amplifies negative self-talk is counterproductive).
- **What to write:** Pick **one** meaningful event. (1) What did I intend? (2) What happened? (3) Why the gap? (4) Sustain / Improve — end with **one** improve written as an if-then.
- **Filled example:** *"Intended: 9am deep-work block. Actual: lost it to Slack. Why: phone within reach. Sustain: afternoon phone-in-drawer worked. Improve: IF I sit down at 9, THEN phone goes in the drawer first."*
- **Compounds:** Each day's "improve" is a tested micro-experiment; ~66 self-experiments accumulate into a personal pattern library of your systematic errors.

#### N3 — Calibrated Prediction Log  (reasoning / forecasting) — ~5–6 min
- **Technique & source:** **Tetlock & Gardner, *Superforecasting* (2015)** and the **Good Judgment Project** (IARPA, 2011–2015); scored with the **Brier score**. **[Confirmed** — the one thinking skill with tournament-grade evidence that practice-with-feedback improves it**]**
- **Mechanism:** Putting a *number* on a belief and *scoring it later* is the only way to get feedback on judgment — vague opinions can't be wrong; probabilities can. Superforecasters averaged a Brier ~**0.166** vs ~**0.259** for regular forecasters, and even ~1 hour of training measurably improved accuracy; the top trait is **"perpetual beta"** (relentless updating).
- **What to write:** One falsifiable prediction + probability (e.g., 70%) + resolution date. Resolve any due ones. (Brier math happens weekly.)
- **Filled example:** *"Client signs by Fri — 65%. Resolves Jun 12. [Resolved: 'rain Tues, 80%' → ✓]"*
- **Compounds:** A scored track record. By Day 66 you can read your calibration curve — do your "70%s" happen ~70% of the time? This is the most measurable skill in the system, and it's the main reason 66 days beats 50: calibration needs weeks of resolved forecasts to mean anything.

#### N4 — Consider-the-Opposite (+ occasional 2-hypothesis check)  (de-biasing) — ~5 min, most days
- **Technique & source:** **"Consider-the-opposite"** (Lord, Lepper & Preston, 1984) — the best-supported debiasing move — paired with a *lightweight* two-hypothesis version of **Heuer's Analysis of Competing Hypotheses** (*Psychology of Intelligence Analysis*, CIA, 1999). **[Confirmed** for consider-the-opposite **/ Practitioner** for ACH, with a validity caveat: controlled tests of full ACH show little-to-no benefit — Dhami & Mandel, 2019**]**
- **Mechanism:** Directly counters confirmation bias — the default failure mode of judgment (Kahneman, 2011). Full ACH matrices take analysts hours and don't reliably help, so we use the cheap, evidenced core: argue the other side and name the evidence that would change your mind.
- **What to write:** Take one belief/decision from today → the **strongest case against it** → one piece of evidence that *would* change your mind. (Once or twice a week, expand to two competing hypotheses + the single most diagnostic test between them.)
- **Filled example:** *"Belief: the slow barista was just tired. Opposite: the café's understaffed (saw one person all week) — a pattern, not a mood. Evidence to check: is it slow again tomorrow at the same time?"*
- **Compounds:** Builds the reflex of generating the counter-hypothesis automatically — the move that separates analysts from pundits.

#### N5 — Idea Development via SCAMPER  (creativity) — ~5 min, alternating days
- **Technique & source:** Combinatorial creativity via **SCAMPER** (Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put-to-other-use, Eliminate, Reverse — Eberle, 1971, building on Osborn). **[Practitioner]**
- **Mechanism:** Most ideas are recombinations; SCAMPER forces structured recombination instead of waiting for inspiration.
- **What to write:** Take the day's seed (or a past one) and run it through 1–2 SCAMPER prompts.
- **Filled example:** *"Locker app → COMBINE with the gym's existing membership app; REVERSE: lockers free, but pay to reserve a premium one."*
- **Compounds:** Turns the daily idea-capture from a graveyard into a development pipeline.

### ZONE 3 — WEEKLY REVIEW  (Sundays, ~25–30 min)
*This tier is where **spacing** does its work (Cepeda et al., 2006/2008): re-encountering material after a gap dramatically improves retention; the optimal gap grows with how long you want to remember it.*

1. **Spaced re-read** of the week's captures (the spacing rep). From Phase 4 on, add a **"7-days-ago" and "30-days-ago" recall sweep** before re-reading.
2. **Brier scoring:** tally resolved predictions — over- or under-confident?
3. **Best-idea cull:** pick the single best idea of the week; optionally SCAMPER it.
4. **Pattern synthesis:** "What showed up 3+ times this week?" (the Rule of Three applied to your own data).
5. **Elicitation-in-the-wild note:** one technique you *noticed* (flattery, assumed shared knowledge, a leading statement inviting correction) — builds awareness of information flow and your own susceptibility. **[Practitioner]**
6. **System AAR:** run the four AAR questions on the *habit itself*; adjust. Check the conditional outreach count (2–3?).

> **Night-session time math (full day):** 4 (recall) + 9 (AAR) + 6 (prediction) + 5 (consider-opposite) + 5 (SCAMPER, alt days) = **~29 min**, ~24 on non-SCAMPER days. Comfortably inside 30–45, with slack for a bad night.

## PART 3 — THE 66-DAY PROGRESSION (ramped in phases, deepening not padding)

The ramp respects habit science: start almost-too-easy to lock in the cue, then add complexity only after the behavior is sticky. The arc is built so the **last third spaces and stress-tests** earlier skills rather than introducing busywork. **Recovery rule throughout:** missing one day doesn't reset you (Lally et al., 2010) — just **never miss twice in a row.**

**Phase 1 — Install the anchor (Days 1–14): make it unmissable.**
*Only the core:* D1 Baseline+Anomaly, D2 Three Noticings, and a 2-line AAR (questions 2 and 4 only, ending in one if-then). Fixed cue, fixed place, same time. Goal = *never miss*, not *go deep*. Use Fogg's recipe — "After I [brush my teeth], I will [open the journal]" — and tick it off. *(This is the implementation-intention window; nothing else matters yet.)*

**Phase 2 — Add precision (Days 15–28).**
Add the **daily calibrated forecast** (N3) and **retrieval-first recall** (N1). Add the **people note** D4 (baseline→shift→question). Begin the **full four-question AAR**. Run your **first weekly review** (~Day 21). *Why now:* the prediction log must start early so a scoreable track record exists by Day 66.

**Phase 3 — Add rigor (Days 29–44).**
Add **consider-the-opposite** (N4, most days) and **SCAMPER idea development** (N5, alternating). Begin **Brier scoring** in the weekly review; add **one in-vivo Voss rep/week** (a label or calibrated question in a real conversation). **~Day 35: mid-system self-critique** — cut anything you're routinely skipping (see Part 6 of the system logic).

**Phase 4 — Transfer & calibration (Days 45–60).**
Push skills into the real world: a calibrated question or label in a live conversation **most days**; one **"recall-the-room"** daily; resolve a fuller forecast set. Add **spaced retrieval sweeps** (7-days-ago and 30-days-ago) to the weekly review — this is the deepening the extra days buy you. Begin **conditional give-first outreach** (2–3/week). Add an occasional **two-hypothesis check** when a real decision warrants it.

**Phase 5 — Automaticity check & consolidation (Days 61–66).**
Taper the scaffolding and **measure**: chart your **prediction calibration curve** and your **anomaly miss-rate**; pick your **top 3 ideas** of the 66 days; run a **meta-AAR** on the whole system. Then design your lean **maintenance version** (the non-negotiable core + whichever add-ons earned their keep). Set expectations honestly: with an 18–254-day range, the cue should feel automatic by now, but the full routine may still take some effort — plan to continue.

**The non-negotiable core** (must survive your busiest day): **D1 Baseline+Anomaly + D3 one idea + the 2-line AAR with one if-then.** Everything else is optional load.

## PART 4 — RANKED EXTRA MICRO-DRILLS (2–5 min), rotate 1–2 in
*Ranked by evidence strength × leverage. Don't add them all.*

1. **Pre-decision pause / "Outside View" (3 min) — [Confirmed].** Before any non-trivial decision: "What's the base rate? How have similar cases turned out?" Reference-class forecasting was the GJP training component most correlated with accuracy. *Source: Tetlock & Gardner, Superforecasting (2015); Kahneman (2011). Purpose: calibrated judgment.*
2. **Standalone calibration reps (2 min) — [Confirmed].** Predict trivial things (meeting starts late? 75%) and score them. *Source: Good Judgment Project. Purpose: faster calibration feedback than work-only forecasts allow.*
3. **Consider-the-opposite on one headline (3 min) — [Confirmed].** Take one claim or strong opinion you hold; write the strongest steelman against it. *Source: Lord, Lepper & Preston (1984); Heuer (1999). Purpose: confirmation-bias resistance.*
4. **Recall-the-room (3 min) — [Practitioner].** On leaving any room, look away and reconstruct it: exits, number of people, who stood where, anything out of place. *Source: USMC observation tradition / "Kim's Game." Purpose: Level-1 SA + encoding.*
5. **Negative-space scan (3 min) — [Practitioner].** While walking, force your eyes into the gaps — alleys, shadows, spaces between objects — not the solid masses. *Source: USMC Observation Theory & Optics (B1E0795). Purpose: counters the eye's pull toward positive space.*
6. **Alternate Uses (3 min) — [Confirmed].** Pick an object; list as many uses as possible in 3 minutes. This is the actual lab measure of divergent thinking. *Source: Guilford (1967). Purpose: ideational fluency/flexibility.*
7. **One calibrated question or label, in vivo — [Practitioner].** Replace a statement or yes/no question with a "How…/What…" question, or name an emotion ("Sounds like you're slammed"). *Source: Voss (2016); Navarro (2008). Purpose: tactical empathy / reading the room. (Practitioner wisdom, not RCT-tested; the active-listening and open-question mechanisms underneath are supported.)*
8. **Key-assumptions check (2 min) — [Practitioner].** Write the one assumption that, if wrong, would most change your current plan. *Source: Heuer (1999); CIA Tradecraft Primer. Purpose: surfaces hidden premises — the classic intelligence-failure mode.*
